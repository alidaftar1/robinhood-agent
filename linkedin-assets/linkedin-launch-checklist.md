# LinkedIn Launch Checklist (reusable)

The asset is almost never the bottleneck — the **launch** is. Post #1 (the Robinhood
agent) was a strong post with strong visuals and got ~4 likes, because it went out at
8pm PST Wednesday with no seeding. That's a dead-zone launch, not a content failure.

The LinkedIn algorithm shows a new post to a small test slice of your network (~5–10%),
measures engagement *rate* and *dwell* in the first ~60–90 min, then decides whether to
expand reach. Weak first-hour velocity = throttled before most of your network ever sees it.
Everything below exists to win that first hour.

---

## TIMING — when to post
- **Day:** Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Never Friday–Monday. Never weekends.
- **Time:** ~**8am PT (11am ET)**. Captures East Coast late-morning + West Coast morning,
  and you're awake to engage. 
- **Never** evenings (post #1's mistake) — LinkedIn is a weekday-daytime platform.
- Post when YOU can be fully present for the next 90 minutes. Don't post and walk away.

## SEEDING — line this up BEFORE you hit post
- Pre-arrange **5–8 peers** (tech/AI/PM — credible accounts, NOT family).
- The moment it's live, DM/text them: *"Just posted — a comment in the next 30 min would
  mean a lot."* Ask for **comments, not likes** (comments are the strongest expansion signal).
- Target **3–5 real peer comments in the first 30–60 min.**
- Family comments (e.g. a sibling) don't help and slightly hurt — they signal "personal post,"
  not "professional resonance." Make sure peer comments land too.

## FIRST 90 MINUTES — be on-platform and active
- Reply to **every** comment with a **follow-up question** (sub-threads boost reach more
  than a bare "thanks").
- Like and reply fast — velocity in the first hour is what the algorithm tests.
- Engage on a few *other* people's posts right before/after posting (warms your reach).

## THE POST ITSELF
- **Hook (first 1–2 lines):** must work before "see more." Lead with a result, a number,
  a confession, or a contrarian claim. (Post #1's "losing to SPY… and that's not the most
  interesting part" was strong — keep that bar.)
- **Lead with the payoff**, don't bury it. If the hook poses a question, answer it.
- **One story, not a résumé.** Cut the technical war-stories to your 2 best, punchy ones.
  Dense jargon (T+1, LLM-as-judge, cron caps) makes non-engineers bail before the CTA.
- **CTA = one question anyone can answer in 5 seconds.** Broad beats niche for comment
  *volume*. ("Would you let an AI trade your money?" pulls everyone; "what failure mode
  surprised you building agents?" only pulls people who've built agents.)
- **Link in the FIRST comment, not the body.** External links in the body suppress reach.
  (Post #1 did this right.)

## VISUALS
- **Mobile-first: ~70% of views are on a phone.** If text is unreadable at thumbnail size,
  it won't stop a scroll. Post #1's architecture/reasoning/evals images were walls of tiny
  text — only the dashboard read at a glance.
- **Lead image = your most legible, most story-telling asset.** (For this project: the
  dashboard chart.)
- **Bake a bold callout into the lead image** — big text with the key number/claim. Don't
  rely on small UI text to carry the punch.
- **Dramatize the money shot** — annotate the chart (arrow on the divergence, big return %).
- **Format:** native PDF/document carousel > a pack of separate images (more dwell + reach).
- **Cut weak assets.** A grid of "100% / 100% / 100%" evals reads as trivial or
  too-good-to-be-true to outsiders. Drop it or replace with one zoomed, legible example.

## REFRAME — don't over-read one post
- A single post's engagement is mostly noise: timing, who's online, early velocity, luck.
  Don't conclude the work or the writing is weak from one flat post.
- **Consistency is the growth engine.** Serialized "here's the next result" content compounds
  the audience over weeks. The first post rarely pops.

---

## Pre-publish checklist (run every time)
- [ ] Tue/Wed/Thu, ~8am PT
- [ ] 5–8 peers lined up to comment, DMs drafted
- [ ] Hook works before "see more"
- [ ] Payoff/result is upfront, not buried
- [ ] Technical middle trimmed to 2 punchy stories
- [ ] CTA is one easy, broad question
- [ ] Link ready to drop in the FIRST comment
- [ ] Lead image is legible on mobile + has a bold callout
- [ ] Weak visuals (e.g. evals grid) removed
- [ ] You're free to engage hard for the next 90 min
